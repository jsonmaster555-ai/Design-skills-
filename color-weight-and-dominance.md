# Color Weight and Dominance

Color has visual weight. Use it intentionally to communicate state, emphasis, meaning, and brand without allowing color to dominate the entire interface.

Start with a stable neutral foundation. Introduce brand and semantic colors where they provide information or meaningful emphasis.

Saturated colors attract attention quickly. Reserve them for elements that deserve that attention. Do not make every icon, badge, button, card, and heading a different bright color.

Primary actions may use stronger color. Secondary actions can use neutral or lower-emphasis treatments. Semantic colors such as success, warning, and error should communicate actual states rather than decorate ordinary content.

Do not rely on color alone to communicate critical meaning. Pair it with text, iconography, shape, or state treatment where necessary.

Consider occupied area. A large pale surface may carry more visual weight than a tiny saturated indicator. Evaluate the composition as a whole.

Dark mode is not created by simply inverting colors. Rebalance surfaces, text contrast, borders, and accent intensity for the darker environment.

Color should direct attention selectively. If everything is colorful, color stops providing hierarchy.
