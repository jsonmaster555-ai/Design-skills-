# Reading Patterns — F-Shape & Z-Shape

Reading patterns describe common ways users scan an interface before deciding what deserves closer attention. They are tendencies and composition models, not rigid laws.

## F-shaped scanning

F-shaped scanning is especially relevant to text-heavy pages, documentation, search results, settings, long lists, and information-dense interfaces. Users often give more attention to upper content and the leading edge of lines.

Front-load meaningful words in headings, labels, list items, and descriptions. Create scanning landmarks with headings, short paragraphs, labels, lists, highlighted values, links, typography, and spacing.

## Z-shaped composition

Z-shaped composition is useful for simpler layouts with relatively few major elements, such as landing-page heroes, signup screens, onboarding, and product introductions. Attention can move across the upper region, through central content, and toward a lower destination such as a CTA.

Do not move content into unnatural positions merely to draw a geometric Z. Context, value, and action should remain logically connected.

## Hierarchy overrides diagrams

Strong headings, imagery, motion, contrast, and buttons can redirect attention. The stronger visual signal usually wins. Use that deliberately.

Choose composition from the user's task and content density rather than declaring every page an F-pattern or Z-pattern page.
