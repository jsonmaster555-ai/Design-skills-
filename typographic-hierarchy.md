# Typographic Hierarchy

Typography communicates importance through size, weight, spacing, color, line height, placement, and contrast.

Create semantic roles such as Display, Page Title, Section Heading, Subheading, Body, Label, Caption, and Metadata. Repeated roles should reuse the same typography rather than receiving arbitrary new styles.

## Weight

Avoid excessive Bold, ExtraBold, and Black. Prefer Regular for body and supporting text, Medium for controls and labels, and Semibold for important headings or actions. If text feels weak, first adjust size, contrast, position, surrounding space, or competing elements before increasing weight.

## Scale

A restrained product-UI starting scale can use roughly 12px for metadata, 13–14px for compact labels, 14–16px for body/UI text, 18–20px for small headings, and 24–32px for major interface headings. Larger display sizes should be intentional rather than automatic.

## Tracking and line height

Avoid unnecessary positive letter spacing on normal headings and body text. Large headings can use slightly tighter tracking. Small uppercase labels may need additional tracking. Body text needs more line-height than large headings.

## Typeface selection

Prefer established typefaces with clear licenses and strong screen readability. Open-source options include Inter, IBM Plex Sans, IBM Plex Mono, Roboto, Roboto Flex, Noto Sans, Source Sans 3, Source Serif 4, Manrope, DM Sans, Work Sans, and Public Sans. Verify the current license before distribution or embedding.

Do not use monospace for ordinary labels simply to make a product look technical.

Typography should communicate importance without shouting.
