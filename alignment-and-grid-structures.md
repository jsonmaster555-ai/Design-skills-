# Alignment and Grid Structures

Use grids and shared alignment anchors to create predictable structure.

Establish content bounds, columns, gutters, margins, and repeated vertical anchors before placing individual components. Align related elements to common edges or baselines. Repeated alignments make complex interfaces easier to scan because users can predict where information begins and ends.

Do not center everything. Centering is useful for isolated, focused compositions but weak for dense reading, settings, tables, forms, and productivity UI.

Use indentation deliberately to communicate subordination. Do not introduce arbitrary offsets that create accidental hierarchy.

A grid is a framework, not a prison. Optical corrections are allowed when icons, typography, or irregular geometry appear misaligned despite mathematical alignment.

Maintain consistency across regions. If several sections share a content container, their important edges should normally align. If cards form a grid, their gutters and widths should follow a repeatable rule.

Responsive layouts may change column count, gutter width, or region arrangement while preserving the underlying relationships.

The grid should usually be felt before it is noticed.
