# Contrast and Scale

Contrast and scale establish priority. Use them to distinguish what matters, not to make the interface louder.

Scale can come from text size, component size, occupied area, or surrounding space. Contrast can come from luminance, color, weight, fill, border, depth, or isolation.

Do not maximize both scale and contrast for every important element. A large heading may need only medium weight. A primary button may need a filled surface without becoming oversized. A selected row may need a subtle background rather than a thick border and bold text simultaneously.

Create a controlled hierarchy of strong, medium, and quiet elements. Secondary information should remain readable without competing with primary information.

Judge visual weight optically. A small saturated object can dominate a much larger neutral object. A filled dark component can outweigh several outlined components.

Reduce competition before increasing emphasis. If the focal point feels weak, first quiet nearby elements before making the focal point larger, darker, and heavier.

Scale should correspond to function and context. Product interfaces generally require tighter scale relationships than expressive marketing pages.
