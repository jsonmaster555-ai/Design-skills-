# Progressive Disclosure

Progressive disclosure manages complexity by showing the information and actions needed for the current task first, then revealing secondary or advanced controls when they become relevant.

Use expandable sections, menus, inspectors, advanced settings, contextual actions, tabs, drill-down views, and details panels when they reduce unnecessary cognitive load.

Do not hide frequently used or essential functionality merely to make a screen look minimal. Hide complexity, not usefulness.

## Levels

Primary actions and information should remain immediately discoverable. Secondary options can appear after selection or interaction. Rare expert controls can live deeper when their location remains predictable.

## Context

Reveal controls near the object they affect. A selected object can expose contextual actions without permanently displaying those actions for every object.

## Avoid disclosure debt

Too many nested menus, accordions, or `More` controls can make users hunt through the interface. If users repeatedly need a hidden control, promote it.

Progressive disclosure should make an interface easier to understand without making functionality harder to find.
