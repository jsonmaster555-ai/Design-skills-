# Scannability Fields

A scannability field is a region whose structure lets users understand its contents through rapid visual scanning rather than careful reading.

Design fields around predictable anchors: leading edges, repeated columns, aligned labels, headings, values, icons, and actions.

Lists should place equivalent information in equivalent positions. Tables should preserve column meaning. Settings should repeat label-control relationships. Cards in the same family should expose comparable information in comparable locations.

Use typographic contrast sparingly to create landmarks. Front-load meaningful words in headings and labels. Keep important values easy to locate.

Avoid random alignment, inconsistent card anatomy, irregular control placement, and excessive decorative variation. These force users to re-learn every item.

Dense fields can still be highly scannable when alignment and repetition are strong. Spacious fields can be difficult to scan when elements float without anchors.

Test the field by looking at it without reading the copy. Its groups, repeated structures, important values, and available actions should still be apparent.
