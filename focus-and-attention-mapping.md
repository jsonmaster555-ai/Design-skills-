# Focus and Attention Mapping

Before finalizing a screen, map the expected order of attention.

Identify the intended first fixation, second destination, primary action, supporting information, and low-priority regions. Then inspect whether size, contrast, position, spacing, imagery, motion, and color actually produce that order.

Attention is competitive. Large images, faces, saturated colors, dark filled surfaces, animation, oversized typography, and isolated objects can override the intended hierarchy.

Do not place a decorative asset beside critical content without evaluating which one wins. Do not animate secondary information continuously. Do not use high-contrast badges everywhere.

The primary focal point should usually be singular within a local region. Secondary elements can create a path away from it.

Map attention according to task. A dashboard may prioritize current status and next actions. A form should prioritize the active field and progression. A landing hero should prioritize proposition, explanation, and action.

When the attention map feels chaotic, remove or quiet competing signals instead of adding another signal.
